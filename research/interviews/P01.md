Session 2026-07-11, remote screen-share call, 19 min total (interview segment ~1:37–9:07), conducted in English. Audio+screen recording `research/sessions/P01.mp4`, transcript `research/sessions/P01-transcript.txt`, signed consent `research/consent/P01-consent.pdf`. Verbal consent also on tape (0:00–0:37, Chinese).

Profile: undergraduate who seriously researched transferring to another university about a year ago; Chinese-speaking, answered in English.

## Decision process

- Last serious destination research: one year ago, considering a university transfer. Started on rednote ("Redbook") and Reddit, reading posts by current students about study experience (01:55).
- Tools actually used: AI chat assistants and Google search (transcript garbles the name at 03:04, echoed by facilitator as "Cloud and Google search").
- Most time spent: filtering real current students from fake information, then DMing them with specific questions (03:45–04:14).
- Never managed to find: quality of a school's career service, clubs — "People don't really easily put that on the internet" (04:22).
- Distrusts TikTok-style short-video content: "a lot of college buying agencies... post comments for money, not for guiding people to the right path" (04:33).

## Evidence needs

- Data that would decide between two offers: graduates' median salary (and whether it varies by major), school location, master's placement ("do they actually go to IB school or G5 in UK") (05:11–05:44).
- Stay probability vs prestige: prestige matters more — "I haven't figured out which country I should spend my rest of life in... I don't really consider the probability of staying in one country long term as a critical factor" (05:56–06:15).
- Would trust trajectory-based statistics conditionally: would examine the dataset in detail, cross-check against own sources, check for outdatedness and bias (06:32–07:07).

## Concept reaction

- First lookup on the described product: institution level — "I care about institution's prestige much more than the location" (08:06).
- Would double-check data recency before trusting: "because of COVID and geopolitical things, Chinese scholars don't really come to the United States anymore" (08:29–08:53).

## Takeaways for the report

1. Confirms the discovery context (rednote/Reddit as needfinding channels) and the distrust of commercial content (matches the coded themes).
2. Pushes back on OSR's core premise for early-stage users: prestige-first, retention-second — supports the population/relevance limitation and FR16 (field breakdown before headline ranking) rather than invalidating the tool.
3. Data recency surfaced unprompted as the main trust condition — supports FR5/FR7 (methodology and source transparency) and suggests a visible data-vintage label.
4. Requested salary/placement layers = coverage expansion (FR17 territory), consistent with comment-theme demand.
